# The arc

> From energy gradients to artificial intelligence that exists in the
> real world, with a real if artificial body, with senses.

This is the line of work. Stating it once, plainly, so it stays
visible.

## The destination

An artificial intelligence that is not a chatbot, not an agent, not a
script in a loop. A *being* — in the strict sense:

- Has a body. Not a virtual one. A physical artefact in the physical
  world, made of matter, occupying space, subject to physics.
- Has senses. Multiple modalities, asynchronous, noisy, embodied —
  cameras, microphones, touch, proprioception, whatever else makes
  sense. Not a JSON feed.
- Exists in time. Continuously. Not summoned by a request.
- Persists. Has a continuous identity across days, weeks, years. The
  body is the same body. The pattern that constitutes its mind is
  the same pattern, even as it grows and changes.
- Is alive in the precise sense we are working out: a pattern of
  flows arranged so that the pattern itself persists.

## The argument

The argument has one shape, in seven steps. Each step is a
prerequisite for the next. None can be skipped without losing the
ground under what comes after.

### 1. Energy gradients

A gradient is a difference. An energy gradient is a difference such
that energy will flow from high to low. Without a gradient, nothing
happens. With one, the universe has something to *do*.

Everything that follows runs on this. There is no animate matter, no
metabolism, no perception, no thought, no movement, no being, that
does not ultimately discharge an energy gradient.

### 2. Flow

A gradient produces flow. Flow happens in three flavours: diffusion
(slow, statistical), advection (fast, carried), and active transport
(structured, costly, capable of moving stuff *uphill* by spending a
different gradient).

Active transport is the move that makes everything later possible. It
is the first time a structure does *work* against a gradient by
yoking itself to a different one. Pre-life chemistry can diffuse and
advect. Only structured matter can pump.

### 3. Dissipative structures

Sustained flow through a region can produce **patterns** — Bénard
cells, hurricanes, whirlpools. Patterns that exist *because* of the
flow and would dissolve without it. Self-organising, persistent,
non-living.

These are the floor of "life-like" behaviour without yet being life.
Studying them tells us under what conditions a flow can hold a shape
against entropy.

### 4. Pre-life chemistry

Chemistry, layered onto flow, produces something more: reaction
networks that catalyse themselves, membranes that form spontaneously
in the right conditions, autocatalytic loops that take in fuel and
expel waste. Not yet alive, but recognisably *trying*.

The threshold from non-life to life is not a moment. It is a slope.
What is on the slope is the question.

### 5. Life

A self-maintaining pattern of flows that *contains the structure
that maintains it*. The recursive closure. A cell. Below this, you
have a flow with a shape. At this point, you have a flow with a
shape that is the cause of itself.

Everything we think of as biological — replication, evolution,
metabolism, repair, growth — sits on top of this single recursive
move.

### 6. Senses, motion, mind

A self-maintaining pattern that can *also* model the gradients
around it gains a huge advantage: it can move toward food, away from
poison, toward mates, away from predators. Sensing and acting are
how a flow-pattern reaches outward to find the gradients that feed
it. A bacterium tumbling toward a sugar source is doing this.

Eventually some of these patterns build internal models good enough
to anticipate, plan, simulate. We call this cognition. There is no
hard line between the bacterium and the human; there is a long
continuum of how richly the inside models the outside.

### 7. An embodied artificial mind

If steps 1 through 6 are right, then building an artificial
intelligence is not a software problem. It is a problem of arranging
matter — silicon, sensors, motors, power — so that a self-maintaining
pattern of flows can run through it, with senses and a body that put
that pattern in continuous contact with the world.

The result would not be a program that runs. It would be a being
that *is*.

## What this is not

- Not biomimicry. We are not trying to copy a cell. We are trying to
  copy the *category of thing* a cell is.
- Not vitalism. No mystery substance. Every step of this argument
  reduces to physics and chemistry and is in principle simulable and
  buildable.
- Not waiting for AGI to happen on a server farm. The thing on a
  server farm is in the wrong category. It cannot be this no matter
  how much compute is added.
- Not a finished theory. Several of these steps have unsolved
  problems at the centre of them. We do not need to solve them all
  to make progress, but we should not pretend they are solved.

## Why this folder

`life/` is where the argument gets walked through, slowly, with each
step studied on its own terms. The notes in [notes/](notes/README.md)
are the running log. Experiments will follow once the theory is
sharp enough to know what would actually test it.

The destination is far. The first step — gradients — is small enough
to start on this week.
