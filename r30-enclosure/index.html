R30 — Enclosure

Phase E, fourth rung. R27′ established real chemistry. R28′ showed it can be transported. R29′ showed it can converge. R30 closes the loop: a self-maintained boundary that contains a wave inside it, paid for by an open-system fuel flow.

The setup. Two species fields on top of the Barkley wave: X (Schlögl bistable) and B (fuel). The Schlögl rate uses local B in place of the constant reservoir term, so the chemistry is bistable only where B is high enough (k4·B ≳ 5.5). B is clamped to a supply value at the grid boundary (the open-system "outside"), decays at a first-order rate everywhere (the downhill paid in chemical work), and is carried inward by a radial-inward velocity field via advect_by. Steady state: B is high in a ring at the edge, deeply depleted in the core.

What you should see. X is seeded high in a 6-cell ring at the boundary. In the ring, B is saturated → Schlögl is bistable → X stays at its high fixed point. In the core, B is depleted → Schlögl is monostable at the low fp → X stays at 1. The high-X ring drives ε high there, which kills the Barkley wave: the spiral inside is enclosed. Now press Cut supply: B at the edge drops to 0, the inward flow carries the deficit through the ring, the bistable's high fp disappears, and the wall drains within ~50 t.u. The wave is no longer enclosed because there is no longer anything to enclose it.

the claim

Structure that pays for itself. The boundary is not painted onto the grid; it is a fixed point of a real bistable reaction running on a real spatial fuel field. B costs first-order decay everywhere; B is supplied at the edge; the inflow rate has to equal the consumption rate for the ring to stay above the bistability threshold. While that balance holds, X sits at its high fixed point in the ring on its own (no comparator, no clamp on X). The moment B falls, so does the wall.

Enclosure is a consequence. The wall raises ε wherever X is high (via modulate_parameter, the same coupling as R27′). High ε makes the Barkley substrate non-excitable. A spiral seeded inside hits the wall, dies at the boundary, and is reflected inward. There is no explicit "wave kill zone" anywhere — the kill zone is a side effect of the chemistry's commitment.

CLI verified at default settings (128×128, |v|=1, λ_B=0.1, B_supply=1.0, wall 6 cells thick): supply on, steady state — wall fraction 18.2 % of ring cells, 0.0 % in the core, ⟨B⟩ ≈ 0.32, core ⟨B⟩ ≈ 0.12, wave alive inside (excited ≈ 0.21). Cut supply at t = 160; by t = 180 wall fraction is 0.0 %; by t = 220 ⟨B⟩ ≈ 0. The wall does not persist without flow — the honest "spends a downhill flow" property.

try this

  1. Default. Watch the four panels. The wall is the X=3 ring; B is brightest at the edge and falls toward the centre; ε is bright exactly where X is. The wave bounces inside.
  2. Cut supply. Press the button or drag the slider to 0. B drops at the edge, the inward flow drags the deficit through the wall, and the wall fades. Within a few seconds the wave fills the grid.
  3. Restore supply. Bring it back to 1. The fuel refills the ring. But X stays at the low fp now — the wall does not rebuild itself from nothing; bistability does not reseed without a kick past the separatrix. Press Reset wall + fuel to re-seed.
  4. Raise λ_B. Consumption faster than inflow → even the ring's B drops below threshold → wall fails even with supply on. Fuel economy matters.
  5. Drop |v| to 0. No inward flow. The supply only reaches the very outermost two cells; the wall there might hold, but the inner part of the seeded ring drains. The "membrane" cannot extend deeper than the perfusion depth.

what this rung is

R30 is the first life/ structure that is honest enough for the ARC's rung 5 (autopoietic membrane). It is not autopoiesis — the wall is not yet self-assembling (we seed it). But it is a closed spatial boundary made of real chemistry, maintained by spending fuel that comes from outside, and it visibly fails when the flow stops. The next rung is to make the wall self-assemble from a single kick: autocatalysis at the rim — that is R31's problem.
