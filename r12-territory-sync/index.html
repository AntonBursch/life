R12 — Territory shapes sync

A third composition. A Cahn-Hilliard concentration field (R8) phase-separates into ± domains. A new operator, bulk_gate, says: where the territory is deep inside one phase (|φ| large), turn coupling on; where it's near the wall (|φ| ≈ 0), turn coupling off. The phase layer (R9) then runs on that coupling map. Each territorial bulk becomes its own Kuramoto cluster. The walls of the territory are walls of synchrony. As CH coarsens, sync coarsens with it.

the claim

R10 used excitable_gate on a Barkley activator. R11 used phase_to_scalar_field in the opposite direction. R12 is a third recipe in the same kitchen: a new operator, bulk_gate, applied to a substrate pair the other two rungs never touched (CH ↔ Kuramoto). The operator is sign-blind by design — both bulk phases of a phase-separated field play the same structural role, and only the wall is special. With three operators and four substrates, the alphabet is now big enough to produce qualitatively different phenomena by swapping pieces in and out.

And the phenomenon itself is interesting: a single global Kuramoto population, but split by territory into independent sync clusters, with the walls of the territory acting as phase domain walls. That is the shape of segmentation. In a cortical-binding reading it looks like "feature regions bound by phase-locking, background suppressed by uncoupling, boundaries enforced by the territory map". In a CV reading it looks like "pixels inside an object share a phase; pixels outside do not; the boundary is where the territory map crosses zero". Same math, two readings.

try this

  1. Kbulk = 8 (default). Wait for the territory to coarsen for ~20s. The phase canvas develops two large monochrome regions, roughly matching the φ regions, with mismatched colours.
  2. Kbulk → 0. The composition collapses to R9 with no coupling. Phase canvas becomes pure rainbow noise even after the territory coarsens. Sync requires the substrate.
  3. Kwall → 1. Now the walls are also coupled. The two domains can talk across the wall and slowly align. Cross-domain alignment climbs. The wall is no longer a sync wall.
  4. Half-width → 0.9. Almost the whole canvas counts as "wall". K-field is mostly Kwall. Sync vanishes. The composition only works when the bulk is sufficiently bulky.
  5. σ → 0.3. Natural frequencies are spread wider. Even at high Kbulk, each bulk's r stops short of 1. The composition inherits R9's phase boundary.
  6. Reseed phases. Territory and K-map are untouched; only the initial phases change. Watch the same territorial sync map re-emerge from different colours.

what to look for

  • Walls are sync walls. The boundaries on the phase canvas line up almost exactly with the zero-crossings on the territory canvas. The composition built a segmentation from two scalar fields and one operator.
  • Independent bulks. The "r in φ>0 bulk" and "r in φ<0 bulk" readouts both climb, while r (global) stays well below them. Two Kuramoto populations sharing one canvas.
  • Sync follows territory. When two small domains merge into one large domain, the phases inside renegotiate to a single blob colour. The merger of CH bubbles drags sync along with it.
  • Three timescales, again. CH coarsening is the slowest. Phase locking is faster. Within each cell, the natural frequency drift is the fastest. The composition lives in the window where those three timescales agree.

why this rung matters

With R10, R11 and R12 in place, three of the four possible arrows between R7/R8/R9/R4 have been written. More importantly, the operator menu has grown from one (excitable_gate) to three. The recipe for future rungs is now clear: pick two substrates, pick a coupling operator that converts the state of one into a per-cell parameter of the other, run them on the same grid. Most of the time the heavy lifting is in the substrate; the operator is a few lines of arithmetic. That is why the ladder composes so cheaply.
