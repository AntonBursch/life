the claim

Add a wind, and the same gradient now drifts as well as spreads. The shape of "flow" depends on which mechanism dominates — diffusion smooths, advection drifts. The Péclet number Pe = v · L / D is the dimensionless knob that says which one wins.

try this

  1. Start with v = 0 (slider in the middle). You get R2 back: a straight line from left down to right. Pe = 0.
  2. Nudge v just above 0. The line tilts slightly — more of the box stays near the inflow value, less near the outflow. The wind is pushing the inflow value in.
  3. Push v further (say 0.2 with D = 0.05). Pe ≈ 800. The field now sits at left for almost the entire box and crashes to right in a thin layer at the outflow wall. Diffusion has effectively been outrun.
  4. Reverse the wind (v < 0). The "inflow" is now the right wall. The same shape mirrors.
  5. Crank D up while v is high. The field relaxes back toward the linear R2 profile. Diffusion is catching up.

what to look for

  • Two regimes, one knob. At |Pe| ≪ 1 the steady state looks linear (R2). At |Pe| ≫ 1 the field clings to the inflow value across most of the box and falls in a boundary layer near the outflow.
  • Total flux still in = out. The two readouts are now v · φ − D · ∂φ/∂x at each wall: convective plus diffusive. Their sum is what stays constant at steady state, even though the diffusive part alone may not.
  • Mass through-flow scales with v. Double the wind, double the throughput at fixed boundaries. This is the first hint that how fast a system can process its gradient is a separate variable from how big the gradient is.

why this rung matters

R1 told us a closed system flattens. R2 told us a held gradient supports a steady current. R3 says the shape of that current depends on whether the medium itself is moving. Most of biology lives at moderate-to-high Péclet numbers — bloodstreams, mucociliary clearance, axonal transport — and the reason is exactly what you see here: advection moves things much faster than diffusion can across any meaningful distance. Diffusion is fine for two millimetres. Advection is what gets oxygen to your toes.
