R15 — Stripes route sync

Sixth composition. New substrate pair, no new operator. Swift-Hohenberg (R6) self-organises a striped pattern u; bulk_gate (third reuse, after R12 and R14) reads |u| and writes a per-cell Kuramoto coupling map K that is high inside stripes and low between them; Kuramoto (R9) phase-locks along each stripe and drifts in the gaps. The phase pattern inherits the geometry of the substrate that grew it.

the claim

The operator alphabet is reusable across distinct substrates. R12 used bulk_gate with a Cahn-Hilliard territory (smooth ±1 regions). R14 used bulk_gate again in the same role inside a three-layer stack. R15 uses bulk_gate with a Swift-Hohenberg pattern, whose geometry is striped/spotted instead of bulk phase-separated. Same operator, very different spatial structure of K. The Kuramoto layer underneath doesn't care which substrate authored its coupling map; it just locks where K is high.

try this

  1. Default. Wait ~20s. Panel 1 forms stripes/spots. Panel 2 traces the stripe interiors. Panel 3 develops bright bands of phase along the stripes; the gaps stay scrambled.
  2. Kstripe → 0. Coupling collapses everywhere. Panel 3 returns to rainbow noise. The substrate still draws stripes; nothing downstream reads them.
  3. Kgap → 1. Gaps couple too. The stripe-vs-gap distinction in panel 3 fades. rstripes and rgaps converge.
  4. |u|0 very small. Almost every cell counts as "stripe." K is uniformly high; phase locks globally, but the geometric signature is lost.
  5. |u|0 very large. Almost no cell counts as stripe. K is uniformly low; phase drifts everywhere.
  6. r → 0. Pattern formation dies. u flattens, stripes disappear, K goes uniform. Phase has nothing to follow.
  7. σ → 0. Oscillators are identical; they lock trivially. The stripe vs gap distinction in panel 3 weakens because gaps lock too (no detuning to fight).
  8. σ large. Strong detuning. Only the stripe interiors, where K is large enough to beat σ, can lock. The geometric signature in panel 3 sharpens.

what to look for

  • Phase inherits geometry. The bright bands in panel 3 trace the stripes in panel 1 cell-by-cell. The substrate's shape is visible in the downstream layer that knows nothing about Swift-Hohenberg.
  • rstripes ≫ rgaps. Within stripe cells, phases align. Within gap cells, they don't. The global r averages these and hides the structure; the split measurement reveals it.
  • local correlation is high even when global r is low. Neighbouring cells inside the same stripe lock together, but different stripes may settle into different mean phases. Locking is local, not necessarily global — exactly the signature you'd expect when coupling is itself spatial.
  • Reuse without rewrite. No new Rust. bulk_gate is the same function used in R12 and R14. The composition is just a different upstream substrate.

why this rung matters

R10–R14 built up substrate-by-substrate. R15 shows the other direction: hold an operator fixed and swap the substrate. bulk_gate has now seen ±1-blob territories (R12), a stack with the same territory geometry (R14), and now striped patterns (R15) — three distinct spatial regimes, one operator. That is the kind of evidence you want before claiming the alphabet is real.
