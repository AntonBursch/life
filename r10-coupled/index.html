R10 — Coupled substrates

The ladder's first composition. An excitable layer (R7) is running on the same grid as a phase layer (R9). Wherever the excitable layer is currently firing, the phase layer's local coupling shoots up. Wherever the tissue is at rest, the phase layer is nearly uncoupled. The result: synchrony is no longer a uniform property of the canvas, it follows the wave. The phase blob is wherever the spiral is.

the claim

Up to R9 each rung was one substrate doing one thing. R10 is the first time two substrates have to live in the same room. A real brain doesn't fire and lock with one mechanism. Real cardiac tissue doesn't fire and bind with one mechanism. The whole interesting class of phenomena — binding-by-synchrony, gamma rhythms riding on slow theta, segmentation by phase-locking of feature detectors, swarms that flock only where they can see each other — is built out of one substrate gating another. R10 is the smallest possible example of that pattern. Excitation gates coupling. Coupling produces sync. Sync follows excitation.

The composition operator is one function: excitable_gate(u, Klo, Khi, threshold, sharpness). Cheap, monotone, smoothstepped, no learnable parameters. The interesting behaviour is what falls out of it on top of the substrates we already have, not what is built into it. That is the point of a composition: most of the heavy lifting is done by the rungs you already trust.

try this

  1. Khi = 0.5. The gate is weak. The middle canvas darkens to almost nothing. The right canvas is rainbow snow. Sync gates need a real coupling differential to do work.
  2. Khi = 6. Default. The middle canvas now lights up wherever the wave is. The right canvas develops monochrome blobs riding the spiral.
  3. Push Khi to 10. The gated coupling is strong enough to deep-lock the excited region. The colour blob is almost pure monochrome, and persists in the wake.
  4. Slide σ up to 0.5. The natural frequencies are spread wider. The same Khi can no longer hold sync — colour inside the excited region becomes noisy again. The composition has its own phase boundary, inherited from R9.
  5. Slide Klo up. The "baseline" coupling everywhere is now non-zero. The whole canvas slowly drifts toward partial sync. The wave's bonus on top of that is the shape of the excited region. You have just smoothly interpolated between "pure R9" and "pure R10".
  6. Reseed phases. The spiral is untouched; only the phase layer's initial conditions change. Watch the wake regrow with new colours, same structure. The substrate dance is invariant to the phase layer's history.

what to look for

  • Local sync, global incoherence. The readout shows r (global) staying near zero while local correlation climbs above 0.8. This is the signature of structured synchrony — neighbours march together but the whole population is not on one beat. It is the natural mode of a brain.
  • Sync trails the wave. Colour blobs are strongest just behind the spiral tip, not at the tip itself: the wave needs to spend a few timesteps in a cell before the gated coupling can pull its phase into the local consensus. Lag is causality.
  • Refractory zones forget. Behind the wave, where the tissue is in refractory (u low, v still elevated), the coupling drops back to Klo and the local colour blob slowly decoheres. The pattern is not just "where the wave is" — it is "where the wave has recently been, fading out."
  • Three timescales talking. Barkley front speed ≪ phase coupling timescale ≪ natural frequency drift. The composition only works in a window where those timescales agree well enough. Push σ too high or Khi too low and the window collapses.

why this rung matters

R10 is the smallest demonstration that the ladder is a composable system, not a museum of disconnected models. Every later rung will be a composition. R11 will probably be a phase layer modulating a reaction rate. R12 will probably be a conserved chemical creating a coupling landscape. The substrate menu (R4, R7, R8, R9) and the coupling-operator menu (starting with excitable_gate) are now the alphabet. Apps will be sentences written in this alphabet.

More concretely: R10 produces, with three substrates and one gate, exactly the textbook picture of binding-by-synchrony — a candidate mechanism for how cortical areas link "this feature here belongs with that feature there" by transiently locking their phases. Whether the brain actually uses this trick is still debated. That a tissue made of R7+R9+gate does this without being told to is the point.
