R8 — Phase separation

R7 was the rung where pattern moved. R8 is its opposite. The field is conserved — total mass is fixed forever, nothing flows in, nothing flows out. There is no pump. And yet, from a smooth start, the field separates itself into two phases, the boundaries between them slowly merge, and the whole tissue keeps reorganising for as long as you watch. R7 needs nothing maintained because it's an autonomous excitable medium. R8 needs nothing maintained because it's literally rolling downhill in free energy — the only motion is the system relaxing into deeper and deeper wells, never getting to the bottom because mass conservation won't let it. Pattern with territory instead of pattern with direction.

the claim

Imagine a thin film of a binary alloy that you've just quenched from a high temperature where the two metals were happily mixed. Below some critical temperature, the mixture is no longer stable — the metals want to separate. But they can't just teleport apart; they have to diffuse, atom by atom, and the total amount of each metal is fixed. The only thing that can change is the local concentration c. The free energy of the film has two equal wells, at c=−1 (all of metal A) and c=+1 (all of metal B), separated by a hump at c=0 (the uniformly mixed state). The uniform state is balanced on the hump. A tiny ripple is enough to start it sliding down.

Two competing effects then sculpt the field. The bulk energy wants every point to be in one of the two wells — so concentration values near zero are pushed outward, toward ±1. The interface energy wants gradients to be small — so it punishes sharp boundaries between regions. The system balances these into walls of a fixed width. And then the slowest, most patient process begins: small domains, which have a lot of wall per unit volume, leak material through the surrounding bulk to nearby larger domains, which have less. Small domains shrink and vanish; large domains grow. Average domain size grows like t1/3. The field is always mid-organisation. It never finishes.

try this

  1. Reseed (zero mean). Start from a near-uniform field with tiny zero-mean noise. The canvas is almost flat. Within a few frames a fine spinodal grain appears — pixel-scale dots and worms — then the worms thicken into a bicontinuous labyrinth, then the labyrinth's smallest features start dissolving into their neighbours.
  2. Watch the mass drift. The mass drift readout shows how much the mean has wandered from its initial value. It should stay glued to zero to about 14 decimal places, no matter how long you run. This is the conservation law made visible.
  3. Watch the free energy. It only ever falls. There is no resurgence, no oscillation, no excitation. Every coarsening event removes a little interface and the energy drops.
  4. Reseed (biased +0.3). Now there's more of one phase than the other. The minority phase forms isolated droplets instead of a labyrinth, and the droplets coarsen by Ostwald ripening — small ones evaporate, big ones grow.
  5. Pull surface tension up. Drag κ from 1.0 toward 2.0. Walls get thicker, the labyrinth gets coarser at any given time, mergers slow down. The whole movie plays out in slower geometry.
  6. Pull surface tension down. Drag κ toward 0.3. Walls get razor-sharp, the pattern gets visibly finer, and the system separates faster. At very small κ you start to push the explicit solver — you'd need a smaller dt to stay accurate.

what to look for

  • No drive. Nothing pumps. The film is just sitting there. The motion you see is entirely the system relaxing into deeper free-energy wells while obeying mass conservation. R7's spirals run on the standing energy in resting cells. R8's coarsening runs on the gradient energy stored at interfaces — and uses it up.
  • Conservation made visible. R4 Gray–Scott also has two species, but it's reactive — U can become V. R8's c cannot become anything. The only way the field changes locally is by something moving past its boundary, and the boundary integral is zero on the torus. Mass is invariant by construction.
  • Power-law slowdown. Watch the free energy readout decay. Early on it falls fast; late on it crawls. The characteristic domain length grows like t1/3, which means it takes eight times longer to double a length scale every time you double it. Coarsening is forever — but only because forever is how long it takes to be done.

why this rung matters

R7 made the case that you can have non-trivial pattern with zero external drive, by storing the "drive" in the resting cells themselves. R8 makes the opposite case: you can have non-trivial pattern with zero drive and zero stored excitability — just a free energy and a conservation law. The two extremes bracket a real claim. Most of the systems we care about biologically and cognitively live somewhere in between: partly dissipative (R7-like), partly conservative (R8-like), with the conserved quantities defining what can't happen and the excitable parts deciding what does.

Cahn–Hilliard is also the cleanest available example of "the long, slow tail" — a process that never finishes because what's left to do gets harder as it goes. The developmental biology of any tissue with separating phases (lipid rafts, cell-sorting, polymer blends) lives in this regime. So does, arguably, a lot of long-term memory: not flow, not excitation, just very slowly settling territories.
