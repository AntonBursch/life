R27′ — Bistable death

Phase E opener. R27 made walls irreversible with a comparator (latch_field). Nature has no comparators; nature has bistable reaction networks — mass-action systems whose rate ODE has two stable fixed points. The mitotic switch, the lac operon, sporulation, prion conversion. R27′ uses the simplest one — the Schlögl model — as the substrate for "wall vs. not-wall".

The reaction. A new species X lives on the grid. Two elementary steps: 

A + 2X ⇌ 3X      (autocatalysis)
X ⇌ B            (decay)

with mass-action kinetics 

dX/dt = k₁A·X² − k₂·X³ − k₃·X + k₄B  +  D·[u − u_θ]₊

At (k₁A, k₂, k₃, k₄B) = (6, 1, 11, 6) the bare rate factors as −(X−1)(X−2)(X−3): a low stable state at X = 1, an unstable separatrix at X = 2, a high stable state at X = 3. The last term is the only coupling to the Barkley substrate — the wave activator, where it exceeds u_θ, boosts the autocatalysis rate locally. This is honest chemistry; the wave species pushes the X precursor through the autocatalytic step.

What you see. A passing wave front kicks X past the unstable separatrix in cells it visits enough times. Those cells then fall into the high attractor at X = 3 on their own, with no further input. ε is set proportional to X (high X → unexcitable), so those regions stop firing. Press Kill wave: spiral disappears, X stays high, walls remain. No latch anywhere — just mass-action.

the claim

Same R27 phenomenology, from real chemistry. R27 needed a new operator with its own persistent state (latch_field) to make walls irreversible. R27′ does it with no new state at all — irreversibility falls out of the rate law itself, because the system has two stable fixed points with a finite barrier between them. The chemical attractor does the holding.

CLI verified at default settings (drive 4.0, u_θ 0.8, kill_eps 0.05): X_high fraction plateaus at ~11% of the grid while the spiral runs steady at excited ≈ 0.20. Wave killed at step 12000 (tissue time 240): X_high_frac stays at 0.112 indefinitely; ε_mean stays at 0.0234. The slight drop in <X> (1.292 → 1.225) after the kill is honest mass-action — cells that were partway up the rising side of the separatrix when the wave died fall back to X = 1, while committed cells stay at X = 3.

try this

  1. Default. Watch the spiral lay down its X footprint. The bright cells in panel 2 are ones that have crossed the separatrix; once bright, they stay bright with zero forcing.
  2. Kill wave. Zeros u and v. The wave vanishes. X does not. The high-X regions hold because the Schlögl rate law has a stable fixed point at X = 3 — no comparator needed.
  3. Reseed spiral after killing. The fresh spiral has to live inside the pre-existing high-X regions, which it did not build and cannot easily erase.
  4. Reset chemistry. Slams every cell's X back to 1. This is the chemical analogue of "heal walls" — you can do it from outside, but the substrate itself has no path back.
  5. Lower drive below 2. The wave no longer pushes any cell past the separatrix. X stays at 1 everywhere; the system is just an excitable medium again. The bistability is real but you need real forcing to access it.
  6. Raise u_θ above 1.0. Only the rare cells where the wave peaks above 1.0 accumulate drive. Walls get sparser. The threshold is a real chemical-coupling parameter, not a Schmitt knob.

what changed vs. R27

R27 chain: Barkley → integrate_field(u) → latch_field → modulate_parameter → ε. The latch_field held the wall state.

R27′ chain: Barkley → react_field(X, schlögl + drive) → modulate_parameter → ε. The Schlögl attractor holds the wall state. No comparator, no Schmitt trigger, no per-operator persistent state.

One new operator (react_field) added in this pivot — and it makes a class of operators that were doing chemistry by fiat (latch_field in particular) obsolete. The alphabet contracts where it was bloated.
