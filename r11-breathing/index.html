R11 — Phase drives reaction

The second composition rung. Same alphabet as R10 (Kuramoto phases + Gray-Scott chemistry) but the arrow runs the other way. Here the phase layer is upstream: wherever the phase clock is at its peak, the chemistry's feed rate F is high and patterns grow; at the trough, F is low and patterns starve. When the phase layer locks globally, the whole canvas breathes as one organism. When it doesn't, regions breathe at independent times.

the claim

R10 wired excitation into coupling. R11 wires phase into a reaction rate. Both are the same recipe — one substrate sets a per-cell parameter of another — but they live on different axes, and they produce different phenomena. R10 made sync follow the wave. R11 makes chemistry breathe with the clock. The composition operator this time is even simpler than R10's gate: a smooth cosine map 0.5·(1 + cos θ) from phase to a number in [Flo, Fhi]. No threshold, no sharpness, no parameters — phase has its own native zero-to-one cycle, we just plant it on top of the feed rate.

The interesting thing is what falls out. Gray-Scott has a well-known parameter map: at high F it makes spots; at low F spots dissolve; in a narrow band it makes mazes and stripes. By making F a slow function of time on every cell, R11 walks each cell through this map on the phase period. If neighbours share a phase, they walk through the map in lockstep — a breathing organism. If they don't, you get something more like asynchronous tissue, regions of growth and decay tiled in space.

try this

  1. K = 4 (default). Phases lock locally but not globally; you see patches of synchronously breathing spots, separated by phase walls.
  2. Slide K to 0. Phases are independent. Every cell's feed rate oscillates at its own frequency. The chemistry can't ever settle — it degenerates to noise. Sync was carrying the rhythm.
  3. Slide K to 8 and σ to 0. Identical oscillators, strong coupling. The phase field converges to one global beat. The middle canvas now pulses as a single colour. The right canvas grows a full disk of spots, then starves them off, then regrows. That is one organism breathing.
  4. Slide Fhi down to 0.05. Peak feed is no longer high enough to grow spots anywhere. The right canvas stays nearly empty no matter how synchronised the phase field is. The composition can only do as much as the underlying rung allows.
  5. Slide Flo up to 0.05. Trough feed is now in the spot-friendly regime too. There is no longer a starvation phase. The chemistry stops breathing and just fills the canvas.
  6. Reseed phases. The chemistry's history is preserved; only the rhythm changes. Watch the breathing pattern re-organise on top of the existing pattern.

what to look for

  • Breathing depth. The readout tracks the temporal std of mean(V) over a few hundred frames, normalised by its mean. When phases lock, this number climbs above 0.2 — the whole field is visibly rising and falling. When they don't, it stays below 0.05.
  • Pattern-on-pattern. R11 is not just "make Gray-Scott blink". The spots' spatial pattern still obeys Gray-Scott geometry: corals, dots, stripes depending on the local F. The phase layer modulates which regime each region is in, and when. Two timescales, two pattern generators, one canvas.
  • The phase clock can be slower than the chemistry. Walk the speed slider down: you will see the feed field's colour change slowly across many chemistry steps. Real cortical rhythms live on top of much faster cellular machinery — same shape.
  • No global lock without enough K. Just like R9 had a critical coupling, R11 inherits it. The breathing organism is a phase of the composition, not the only state.

why this rung matters

R10 and R11 are the same coupling pattern run in opposite directions on the same pair of substrates. Each substrate can be a driver or a driven layer. That is the smallest possible demonstration that the substrate menu and the coupling-operator menu are actually composable in the algebraic sense: you can point the arrow either way and the result is meaningful. From here the alphabet expands by adding substrates (R12, R13, …) and by adding coupling operators (phase_to_scalar_field joins excitable_gate in the operator menu). Apps are sentences built out of these two menus.

Concretely: R11 is the textbook picture of a phase-locked metabolism — circadian rhythms modulating gene expression, gamma rhythms gating neurotransmitter release, breathing rhythms shaping blood-vessel reaction. The math is the same in every case: a slow clock multiplies a fast reaction's rate constant. We did it here with one cosine.
