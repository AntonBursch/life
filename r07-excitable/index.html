R7 — Excitable media

Every rung before this made patterns that sit. R4 chemicals, R5 convection rolls, R6 stripes — none of them go anywhere. Excitable media are different. A small kick launches a stereotyped wave that propagates through the tissue. Break the wavefront and the broken end curls into a spiral that keeps spinning. This is the first rung where pattern has direction and history: where a state moves through space like a thought through tissue, leaves a refractory trail behind it, and refuses to die.

the claim

Take a tissue made of cells. Each cell has two knobs: a fast variable that wants to fire when pushed past a small threshold, and a slow variable that lifts the threshold higher right after a firing, then slowly relaxes back. The cell is stable at rest. Nudge it a little and it returns. Nudge it past threshold and it executes a stereotyped excursion: spike, plateau, refractory recovery, back to rest. Couple cells through diffusion of the fast variable and the excursion spreads. That's a wave. Break the wave's leading edge and the broken end can't keep going straight — it curls. The curl chases its own tail. You now have a spiral that pumps fronts through the medium forever, using only the standing energy supplied by the resting cells. The medium is not a pattern-holder. It is a pattern-conductor.

try this

  1. Start at rest. The canvas is uniformly dark. Nothing happens. The medium is alive but quiet.
  2. Drop one kick. Click anywhere on the canvas. A bright circle appears and expands outward as a clean ring wave. The ring has a sharp leading edge and a refractory tail. Where the tail reaches back to the centre, the centre has already recovered, so nothing fires there a second time — the wave moves outward only.
  3. Drop two kicks. Click once, wait, then click again nearby. The second ring will collide with the first and the two annihilate. Excitable waves cannot pass through each other; they share the refractory tissue and cancel.
  4. Make a spiral. Click ⤺ Seed spiral. A broken wavefront sets up. Within a few rotations the broken end finds its geometry and locks into a clean spiral. From now on the medium emits one wave per rotation, forever.
  5. Tighten or slow the recovery. Drag recovery eps. Smaller eps means longer refractory tails, longer wavelengths, slower spirals. Larger eps means tighter spirals and faster turnover. The geometry of the pattern is set by the kinetics of a single cell.
  6. Try a low threshold. Drag threshold b down toward 0.001. The medium becomes hyper-excitable. Tiny perturbations fire. Multiple spiral cores can spontaneously form and tile the box.

what to look for

  • Stereotyped excursion. Every firing cell goes through the same shape, regardless of how hard the kick was. Above threshold the response is all-or-nothing. This is the binary code of a neuron, a heart cell, a slime-mould tendril.
  • Refractory tail. Behind every front is a region that won't fire again until it recovers. That is why waves don't bounce back, why spirals rotate one way and never reverse, why two waves annihilate on collision. The tail is what gives the pattern direction.
  • Spiral as memory. Once the spiral exists, the medium remembers it. Remove the kick that started it; the spiral persists. The only way to kill a spiral is to overrun its core. The medium is now holding information — the existence and position of the spiral tip — entirely in its dynamical state. There is nothing stored anywhere; the running pattern is the memory.

why this rung matters

R4–R6 were pattern in space. R7 is pattern in space-time. The medium does not record what happened in some buffer; it keeps the past alive by literally continuing to do it. Spiral cores are the simplest physical example of a system whose current state depends on its history and whose history cannot be recovered from a snapshot — you need to watch it run. That is the first ingredient of memory, of computation, of thought. Hearts use exactly this substrate to keep beating; arrhythmias are spirals gone wrong. Slime moulds use this substrate to choose between food sources. Brains, at one level of abstraction, are an obscenely sophisticated version of the same trick: a tissue of excitable cells whose travelling patterns are the cognition.
