R16 — Walls route sync

Seventh composition. New operator gradient_magnitude: read where boundaries are, not where bulk is. Same substrate pair as R12 (Cahn-Hilliard + Kuramoto) but routed through the new operator instead of bulk_gate. R12 syncs inside the ±1 bulk regions; R16 syncs on the walls between them. Operator alphabet now has its "differentiate" category.

the claim

R12 took the same Cahn-Hilliard territory and used bulk_gate to make the ±1 interiors of the domains into sync islands; the walls between them stayed incoherent. R16 holds the substrates fixed and swaps one operator. gradient_magnitude reads the same φ and exposes its derivatives — i.e., the walls. The downstream Kuramoto layer doesn't know it. It simply locks wherever K is high. The wall network of R12 has become the sync backbone of R16.

Concretely: this is the first time the alphabet ships an operator whose job is to differentiate. All previous operators (excitable_gate, phase_to_scalar_field, bulk_gate) read field values. gradient_magnitude reads field changes. That is a new primitive on the kit.

try this

  1. Default. Wait ~20s. Panel 1 forms ±1 blobs. Panel 2 traces the blob outlines. Panel 3 gates them into bright wall rails. Panel 4 develops bright bands of phase along those rails; the interior of each blob stays scrambled.
  2. Kwall → 0. No coupling anywhere. Phase canvas returns to rainbow noise. Walls are still drawn upstream, nothing reads them.
  3. Kbulk → 1. Bulk also couples. The wall vs bulk distinction in panel 4 fades. rwalls and rbulk converge.
  4. |∇φ|0 high. Only the sharpest walls trigger coupling. Panel 3 dims to a thin network; panel 4 locks only along the sharpest ridges.
  5. |∇φ|0 low. Almost every cell counts as "wall." K is uniformly high; phase locks globally; the geometric signature is lost.
  6. σ large. Strong detuning. Bulk cells, with K=0, drift wildly. Walls, with high K, still beat σ and lock. The wall/bulk contrast in panel 4 sharpens.
  7. New territory. Same operators, fresh CH initial condition. The whole sync backbone redraws itself around the new wall network.

what to look for

  • Phase rides the walls. Compare panels 2 and 4: bright bands in 4 match bright rails in 2. Sync follows differentiated geometry.
  • Inverted R12. If you remember R12: bulk locked, walls were quiet. Here it's the opposite. Same upstream, same downstream, different operator -- different geometry. That is exactly what a clean operator alphabet should buy you.
  • rwalls vs rbulk. The headline number. Within wall cells, phases align. Within bulk cells, they don't. Global r is low because the wall network forms several locked clusters with different mean phases.
  • Coarsening shrinks the backbone. As CH coarsens, wall length goes down. Panel 4's lit network thins. Sync structure follows the slow substrate's geometry in real time.

why this rung matters

R10–R15 each added a composition. R16 adds an operator. The operator alphabet so far had three "read" primitives (gate by threshold, by phase peak, by absolute magnitude). It now has its first "differentiate" primitive. This is what unlocks phenomena like walls drive things, edges drive things, flux drives things -- all of which need spatial derivatives to even be expressible.
