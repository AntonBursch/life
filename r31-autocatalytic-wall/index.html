R31 — Autocatalytic wall

Phase E, fifth rung. R30 demonstrated that a chemistry-and-fuel boundary holds together as long as the downhill flow keeps paying for it. R31 asks the next question: can the structure appear from local rules and a single kick? Same alphabet, same chemistry. One operator fewer.

The setup. Same X and B fields as R30 (Schlögl bistable for X, fuel field B clamped at the grid edge, advected radially inward, consumed at rate λ_B). At t=0, X is at its low fixed point everywhere — no wall is seeded. A Barkley spiral is kicked once in the centre. From then on, no further intervention. The wave self-sustains; X evolves under local Schlögl dynamics; B evolves under its own clamp / advect / decay loop. ε is held constant at base (no modulate_parameter feedback): the wave is free to keep sweeping through committed cells, which is what we want for autocatalysis to propagate around the ring.

What you should see. As each wave front passes a cell, the drive term lifts X transiently past the Schlögl separatrix at X=2. In the fuel-rich ring (B large), the high fixed point at X=3 exists, so the committed cell stays committed after the wave moves on — Schlögl's own mass-action irreversibility. In the fuel-starved core (B small), no high fp exists, and X relaxes back to 1. The ring fills in from the kick outward as waves keep sweeping; the core stays blank. The wall is the signature of where B is high enough to support it.

the claim

Origin without a designer. The wave is the only thing seeded. The wall arises from local mass- action on X (Schlögl autocatalysis), gated spatially by where B is high enough to support the bistable's second fixed point. No part of the grid is told to be a wall; no comparator fires on a memory; no latch holds a bit. Cells past X=2 fall into the high attractor on their own and stay there because the chemistry says so.

Selectivity is automatic. The same wave sweeps through both ring and core. Only the ring commits, because that is where k4·B sits above ~5.5 and the high fp exists. The core sees the same drive, but with k4·B too low the only stable state is X=1; the wave-induced bump decays back. Geometry of commitment = geometry of fuel.

CLI verified at default settings (128×128, |v|=1, λ_B=0.1, D=8, B_supply=1.0): wave seeded once at t=0; ring fills in from 6.5% (t=4) to 30.0% (t=20) to a steady 33.2% (t=320). Core fraction stays at 0.0% at every checkpoint past t=10. ⟨B⟩_ring ≈ 0.69, ⟨B⟩_core ≈ 0.12 — the fuel selectivity is real.

try this

  • Watch assembly happen. Press "Reset chemistry", then "Seed new spiral". The ring should start blank and fill in over ~10–20 t.u. as the wave fronts reach the high-B annulus and the Schlögl commit gate fires cell by cell.
  • Starve the chemistry. Move the B supply slider to ~0.5. With less fuel reaching the ring, fewer cells can support the high fp; the wall thins. Drop it to ~0.3 and the wall refuses to assemble at all — the wave still sweeps, but no high fp exists for it to commit cells into.
  • Tune the fuel decay rate. Lower λ_B = longer decay length = wider high-B zone = thicker wall. Higher λ_B = sharper ring = thinner wall. The wall width is set by v/λ_B.
  • Cut the drive. Drop D below ~3 and a single wave passage no longer pushes cells past X=2; the wall stops assembling. The separatrix is real; weak kicks bounce off it.
  • Kill the wave once the wall is in. After the wall has formed, press "Reset wave". The wall persists (chemistry, not the wave, is what holds it). Then move the B supply slider to 0 — the wall drains as in R30. Two honest failure modes, both visible.

what the rung does not yet have

ε feedback is off. R30's full coupling (modulate_parameter from X → ε) is re-introduced in R32, which studies whether a self-built wall is stable when it also kills the wave that built it. That is the autopoietic stability question; this rung answers the autocatalytic origin question first.
