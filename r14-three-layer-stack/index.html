R14 — Three-layer stack

Fifth composition. No new operators, no new substrates. Just bulk_gate (from R12) and phase_to_scalar_field (from R11) stacked into one pipeline: Cahn-Hilliard territory routes Kuramoto coupling, Kuramoto phase paces Gray-Scott feed, chemistry blooms. Three substrates, two operators, one phenomenon. The point of this rung is that the alphabet composes end-to-end.

the claim

R10–R13 each picked one operator and one pair of substrates. R14 says: the alphabet itself composes. Plug bulk_gate's output (a coupling map) into Kuramoto; plug Kuramoto's output (a phase map) into phase_to_scalar_field; plug that output (a feed map) into Gray-Scott. Three substrates ride on each other's backs through five lines of glue. The territory at the top has no idea Gray-Scott exists; the chemistry at the bottom has no idea Cahn-Hilliard exists; yet the bloom map at the bottom shows the territory's shape, filtered through phase, written in chemistry.

That is the whole point of having an alphabet. The substrates are local kernels. The operators are local arrows. Compositions are not bespoke code; they are programs in this little language.

try this

  1. Default. Wait ~30s. Watch panels 1–4 settle. Panel 4 should show distinct bloom rates in the two halves of the territory.
  2. Kbulk → 0. No coupling. Phase canvas (panel 2) becomes rainbow noise; feed map (panel 3) follows; chemistry (panel 4) blooms uniformly. The chain is broken at link 1: no segmentation reaches the bottom.
  3. Fhi = Flo = 0.045. Phase still locks per-territory, but the phase-to-feed link is dead. Chemistry blooms uniformly. Chain broken at link 2: territory and phase are still alive but unread.
  4. Kwall → 1. Walls also couple. The two clusters merge. Both halves of the territory pulse together. bloom_split → 0. The segmentation has been welded shut.
  5. kill → 0.075. Chemistry decays too fast to track the slow phase modulation. Bloom map stays empty regardless of upstream signal. Three layers, but the bottom layer's window has slammed shut.
  6. New territory. Same operators, same phases, fresh CH initial condition. Watch the whole bottom panel re-organise around the new territorial geometry.

what to look for

  • Signal flow visible across panels. Whatever shape is in panel 1, panel 2 stamps onto its phase boundaries, panel 3 modulates the feed inside each, panel 4 records the integral. Top-down causation is literally drawn on screen.
  • Bloom split rings. bloom_split rises and falls as the two clusters drift in and out of phase with each other. When they happen to be aligned, the bloom rate is similar; when opposed, one half blooms and the other rests.
  • Coarsening reaches the bottom. When two CH bubbles merge in panel 1, the matching clusters in panel 2 negotiate to a single colour, and the matching regions in panel 4 start blooming in sync. The coarsening of the slowest layer eventually rearranges the fastest layer.
  • Composition cost. The Rust glue between substrates is a handful of lines. The interesting math lives in each substrate and each operator, exactly once.

why this rung matters

With R14, the alphabet has its first real sentence: three substrates strung together by two operators, producing a phenomenon (territorially-segmented chemical bloom timing) that depends on every layer. This is the smallest possible demonstration that the ladder isn't a list of four-line tricks; it's an authoring surface. R15+ can either reuse the same substrates with different chains, or extend the substrate set, or extend the operator set — the rest is combinatorics.
