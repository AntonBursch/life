R17 — Territory carries dye

Eighth composition. New operator advect_by: the kit's first transport primitive. New helper operator gradient_field reads the vector slope of φ. Same Cahn-Hilliard territory as R12, R15, R16 — now treated as a stream function. A 90° rotation of its gradient makes a divergence-free velocity. A passive dye, seeded as stripes, is carried along the streamlines (which are the level sets of φ). The wall network becomes a network of rivers.

the claim

R10–R16 added gating operators: read a field, gate another field. R17 adds the first transport operator. The dye doesn't react, doesn't lock — it moves. advect_by takes a scalar and a velocity, and traces back where each cell came from a moment ago (semi-Lagrangian). It is the operator that makes the kit able to circulate things.

The composition is light on its feet. The territory is the same one we've been using. gradient_field is the vector cousin of gradient_magnitude from R16. Rotating that gradient by 90° (swap the components, flip one sign) turns φ into a stream function: the velocity is divergence-free by construction. The dye therefore can't pile up — it slides along level sets of φ. The wall network — the same network R16 used to route Kuramoto coupling — is now read as a river network.

try this

  1. Default. Watch the first ~60 steps. The horizontal dye stripes warp at every wall and start to follow them. Each domain becomes a closed loop the dye circulates inside.
  2. Velocity scale → 0. Flow stops. Dye freezes in place. Confirms the transport is doing all the work.
  3. Velocity scale up. Streamline topology unchanged — same level sets — but dye follows them faster. Variance erodes faster too: semi-Lagrangian dissipates.
  4. Reseed dye (vertical). Different initial stripes, same flow field. They warp into the same wall network, just from a different starting angle. The territory imposes the geometry.
  5. New territory. Same operators, fresh CH initial condition. A new river network redraws itself; dye finds it.
  6. Watch long-term. After ~500 steps the dye is mostly mean. Bilinear semi-Lagrangian is dissipative — the streamline topology is preserved but features get smoothed. Reseed to see the transient again.

what to look for

  • Dye respects walls. Compare panel 4 to panel 1: dye boundaries align with φ contours, not across them. Walls are stream lines, not barriers in the gating sense — they're carrying things.
  • Mass stays near 1. The readout "dye mass" stays within a few percent of its initial value. Stream-function velocity is incompressible by construction; semi-Lagrangian drifts a little but doesn't blow up.
  • Variance decays. The "var / var₀" number falls over time. That's the cost of bilinear semi-Lagrangian: no diffusion in the PDE, but the numerical scheme adds some. The topology of the dye flow is honest; the contrast erodes.
  • R12 / R16 / R17 trio. All three use the same Cahn-Hilliard territory. R12 made the bulk into sync. R16 made the walls into sync. R17 makes the walls into a flow. Three different downstream operators, three different roles for the same upstream geometry.

why this rung matters

With advect_by on the kit, the operator alphabet finally has a transport category. Until now everything was reaction-like: a substrate evolved in place; an operator gated or read it; a second substrate evolved in place. R17 adds the ability to move state through space. That is the piece you need for fluids, traffic, signal flow, anything where information has to physically travel.

Operator alphabet now has 6 ops in 4 categories: read-by-threshold (excitable_gate), read-by-peak (phase_to_scalar_field), read-by-magnitude (bulk_gate), differentiate-scalar (gradient_magnitude), differentiate-vector (gradient_field), transport (advect_by).

Math + code: flow::CahnHilliard2D, flow::gradient_field (new), flow::advect_by (new, semi-Lagrangian with bilinear interpolation and periodic wrap).
