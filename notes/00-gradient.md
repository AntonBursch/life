# 00 — Energy gradient

> A gradient is a difference. An energy gradient is a difference in
> energy between two places (or two states) such that energy will
> spontaneously flow from the high side to the low side.

## The everyday picture

- A hot cup of coffee in a cold room. Heat flows out of the cup until
  both are the same temperature. The gradient is the temperature
  difference. When it's gone, nothing more happens.
- Water above a dam. Gravitational potential is high at the top, low
  at the bottom. Open the gate, water flows down. When the reservoir
  empties, the gradient is gone.
- A charged battery. Chemical potential is high in one electrode, low
  in the other. Connect a wire, electrons flow. When the chemicals
  equilibrate, the battery is dead.

In all three: **the gradient is the thing that does work.** Once it's
flat, the system is at equilibrium and nothing interesting happens.

## Why this matters for life

The second law of thermodynamics says closed systems run *toward*
equilibrium — toward flat, boring, maximum entropy. A jar of gas
eventually has the same density everywhere. A hot thing in a cold room
eventually matches the room.

Life looks like it violates this. A cell is wildly non-equilibrium:
ordered membranes, sorted ions, structured proteins, low internal
entropy compared to the soup it floats in. How?

The answer: **a cell is not a closed system.** It sits in an energy
gradient — sunlight, food molecules, geothermal heat, redox chemistry
— and it captures some of the flow as that gradient discharges through
it. By dumping heat (high entropy) into its surroundings, it can
locally reduce its own entropy. The total entropy of cell + surroundings
still goes up. The second law is fine.

Said differently: **life is a thing that lives in the flow of a
gradient and steers some of that flow into maintaining itself.** No
gradient → no flow → no life. Flat universe → dead universe.

## The deep claim (Schrödinger, Prigogine, England)

> Life is a **dissipative structure** — a pattern that exists because
> it is dissipating an energy gradient, and would dissolve if the
> gradient went flat.

Not metaphor. Non-living examples:

- **Bénard cells.** Heat a thin layer of fluid from below. Below a
  threshold, heat conducts uniformly. Above it, the fluid spontaneously
  organizes into hexagonal convection cells. The cells exist *because*
  heat is flowing through them. Stop heating — they dissolve.
- **Hurricanes.** A coherent self-maintaining pattern that exists
  because warm ocean water is releasing heat into cold upper atmosphere.
  It pulls itself together to dissipate that gradient *faster* than
  smooth convection would. Cut off warm water → dies in hours.
- **Whirlpools.** Pull the plug. The water organizes into a vortex
  that drains faster than disorganized flow.

Each is a hint at life: structure emerges, persists, and even competes
for gradient — without anything alive, without genes, without
replication. Just physics in a flow.

The provocative version (Jeremy England, ~2013): take a system of
particles with energy flowing through it; configurations that are
*better at dissipating that energy* are statistically favoured to
persist. Over enough time, you get patterns that look increasingly
life-like, just from being good at eating gradients.

## Why this might be the floor

The other candidates from [life/README.md](../README.md) all
presuppose a gradient and a flow:

- **Boundaries** — a membrane is a thing that maintains a chemical
  gradient across itself. No gradient to maintain, no reason for the
  membrane.
- **Self-replication** — copying requires work, which requires a
  gradient to draw from.
- **Autocatalysis** — a closed reaction loop only persists if reactants
  keep arriving and products keep leaving — a chemical gradient driven
  through it.
- **Compression** — finding regularities is metabolically expensive;
  you need a flow to pay for the cognition.
- **Persistence under perturbation** — recovering from a kick requires
  injecting energy back in, which requires a gradient.

The gradient is what they all run on. It might be the only thing that
has to be there before anything else can be.

## What pre-life looks like under this lens

- Sun heats one side of a rock; cold space cools the other. Temperature
  gradient. **Nothing alive.**
- A hydrothermal vent: hot mineral-rich water from below meets cold
  ocean above. Chemical and thermal gradient. Mineral structures
  precipitate. **Still nothing alive.**
- Some pore networks happen to make products that self-organise into
  membranes that locally trap more of the flow. Those pores **dissipate
  the gradient better** than empty pores. They persist longer. They
  grow. **This is the moment the line gets blurry.**

The transition from non-life to life is not a sudden event on this
view. It's a continuous shift along **how good a structure is at
capturing and exploiting a flow.** A rock is bad at it. A hurricane
is medium. A bacterium is extraordinary.

## Experiment seed

Before building "a creature," build **a flow**: a box with a
high-energy side and a low-energy side, energy passing through. Watch
what does and doesn't form. Vary the rate of inflow. Look for the
threshold above which organised structure appears.
