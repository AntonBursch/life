R13 — Spikes seed pattern

A fourth composition. A Barkley excitable layer (R7) runs spiral waves. Wherever the activator u is firing, excitable_gate raises the per-cell Gray-Scott feed (R4) into the mitosis regime; wherever it's at rest, the feed sits in the stable-spots regime. Chemistry is alive everywhere — but it blooms in the wake of the wave. The spiral writes onto the chemical canvas.

the claim

R10 used excitable_gate on Kuramoto. R12 used bulk_gate on Kuramoto. R13 returns to excitable_gate but points it at a different downstream substrate: Gray-Scott chemistry. One operator, two completely different effects, depending on what it's wired to. That is the alphabet thesis: the operators are real primitives, not one-off glue.

And the phenomenon itself is the cleanest two-timescale story so far. Barkley moves fast — a wave crosses the grid in seconds. Gray-Scott moves slow — pattern grows over minutes. The composition is a writer/memory pair: the fast layer paints transient feed pulses; the slow layer integrates them into a persistent texture. In a CV reading: a fast event detector (spiral as "motion detected") triggers a slow accumulator (chemistry as "heatmap of activity").

try this

  1. Default. Wait ~30s. The spiral runs steadily; chemistry slowly fills regions the spiral sweeps. After a minute, V coverage reaches 30–40%.
  2. Fhi → 0.040. Now firing cells get the same feed as resting cells. The composition collapses: pattern grows uniformly, ignoring the spiral. The wave is decoupled from the memory.
  3. Flo → 0. Resting cells are starved. Pattern only persists in regions recently swept by the wave; once the wave wanders off, that region decays. The chemistry becomes a fading trail behind the front.
  4. kill → 0.075. Chemistry decays faster. Even with vigorous firing, V can't accumulate. The memory has too short a half-life to keep up with the writer.
  5. spike threshold → 0.8. Only the very tip of the wave front qualifies as "firing". The feed pulse is now a thin contour following the wave. Pattern grows along narrow tracks.
  6. Reseed chemistry. Wipe V, leave spiral and feed running. Watch the same pattern regrow — the spiral is the deterministic source.

what to look for

  • Wave wakes, not wave fronts. Pattern doesn't hug the wave front; it grows behind it, where the feed pulse has had time to feed the chemistry but the wave has moved on. Two timescales, written on top of each other.
  • The arm of the spiral as a brush. As the spiral arm rotates, it sweeps the same region periodically. That region accumulates more pattern than the centre or the far corners.
  • Operator reuse. excitable_gate already gated Kuramoto coupling in R10. Here the same five lines of code gate a different parameter of a different substrate — and the resulting phenomenon looks nothing like R10. The operator is the noun; the substrate is the verb.
  • Memory vs. event. The spiral itself is unchanged; reset chemistry and the spiral keeps going, ready to repaint. The memory layer doesn't feed back. (That would be R14.)

why this rung matters

The R7 → R4 arrow is now drawn. Of the four substrates in the alphabet (R4, R7, R8, R9), every one now appears on at least one side of a composition. The operator menu has three entries (excitable_gate, phase_to_scalar_field, bulk_gate), and each operator now appears in at least two compositions. The ladder is no longer a line of one-offs; it's a small grammar.
