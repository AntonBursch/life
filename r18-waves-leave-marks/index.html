R18 — Waves leave marks

Ninth composition. New operator threshold_event: the kit's first discretiser — it turns a continuous field trajectory into a per-cell event mask. A Barkley excitable medium (same as R7) propagates spirals; every step the operator looks at u against a threshold and emits a 1 wherever u just rose through it. A counter accumulates events per cell; a last-fire field decays into a trace. Continuous waves come in; discrete spikes come out.

the claim

R10–R17 added operators that take fields and give back fields. R18 adds the first operator whose output is not a field. threshold_event takes two snapshots of u — before and after — and produces a per-cell mask of 1's exactly where u crossed a threshold upward. The mask is symbolic: a point event in space-time. Downstream this is what you feed into spike counters, latches, schedulers, anything discrete.

The composition is small but the category is new. Continuous waves on a 2D substrate get sampled into point events; events accumulate into rates; rates are statistics over a discrete process born from a continuous one. That is the bridge between the reaction-diffusion world and the event-driven world. A neuron isn't a field; it is a field that fired.

try this

  1. Default. Watch the trace panel (3). The spiral arm has a glowing tail in the event-trace field — that's its history. Compare to panel 1: panel 1 is the wave; panel 3 is the wake.
  2. Threshold = 0.1. Every cell fires as the wave rises early. Coverage shoots to 100% fast; cumulative grows. Lower threshold, more sensitive detector.
  3. Threshold = 0.85. Only the peaks of u cross. Many cells stop firing. Coverage may plateau well below 1. High threshold, stricter detector.
  4. τ → 0.5. The trace collapses onto the events themselves — almost instantaneous forgetting. τ → 30: trace persists like a long-exposure photograph of the spiral.
  5. Watch the count map (4) over time. After several thousand steps, the cumulative count map is a histogram of "how many wavefronts visited me". For a stable spiral it's nearly uniform — every cell sees the wave once per period.
  6. Kick centre. Inject a fresh excitation. New target waves appear and create a new band of events.

what to look for

  • Event mask hugs the wavefront. Panel 2's lit pixels in each frame trace the leading edge of u, where u just crossed θ. The back of the wave (u falling) is dark — only rising crossings fire.
  • Cumulative grows linearly. Once the spiral is stable, the cumulative event count grows at a steady rate (cells × passes / period). The "mean rate" readout converges.
  • Counts equalise. Given long enough time, the count map (panel 4) goes nearly flat — a single rotating spiral hits every cell the same number of times. The non-uniformity comes from the spiral core, which is special.
  • R7 vs R18. R7 showed the continuous activator. R18 listens to it. Same substrate, new role: source of discrete events rather than continuous reagent.

why this rung matters

With threshold_event the operator alphabet finally has a discretise category. Until now everything was field-in / field-out. R18 adds the bridge to point processes — to spikes, triggers, alarms, transitions. That is the machinery you need for everything event-driven: neural readouts, control-loop edge detectors, counters, logic.

Operator alphabet now has 7 ops in 5 categories: read-by-threshold (excitable_gate), read-by-peak (phase_to_scalar_field), read-by-magnitude (bulk_gate), differentiate-scalar (gradient_magnitude), differentiate-vector (gradient_field), transport (advect_by), discretise (threshold_event).
