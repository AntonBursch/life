R29′ — Bistable convergence

Phase E, third rung. R28′ showed one source can deliver commitment downstream via reactant transport. R29′ asks what happens when two such streams meet. Same chemistry as R27′/R28′, same operators as R28′, one knob different: the velocity field reverses sign at the midline.

The setup. Two disks of radius 6 at (W/5, H/2) and (4W/5, H/2) hold X clamped at X = 3 (an honest Dirichlet boundary condition representing continuous metabolic supply). The velocity field is vₓ = +v on the left half, −v on the right half, so both reservoirs flow toward the midline. The Schlögl reaction runs locally; the wave adds its drive where it peaks.

What you should see. The midline band reaches X > 2 commitment faster and higher than either source disk's surrounding half — because cells near the midline receive inflow from both directions. That is the convergence property. Then press Release sources: with no further input, advection carries committed X away faster than the Schlögl rate can hold it, and the whole grid drains back to X = 1. That is the honest finding R29 obscured.

the claim

Real-reactant communication composes. Two streams of the same species converge on a single chemical accumulator, lifting it past its bistable separatrix from both sides. The midline commits at a higher level than either flank — the channels OR. Same alphabet as R28′, position-dependent transport only.

Open systems forget. When the sources stop, chemistry alone can't hold the midline. Advection sweeps committed cells away faster than the Schlögl rate can stabilise them; without a fresh inflow, even cells past the separatrix drop back to the low attractor as their X is carried out. R29 (with latch_field) showed permanent walls after sources died — that was a bookkeeping trick. R29′ shows what nature actually does: persistence requires real continuous supply.

CLI verified at default settings (|v| = 2, drive 4, sources radius 6): while clamped, X_hi_mid plateaus at 13.5 % versus X_hi_L = X_hi_R = 9.1 % — the midline commits roughly 50 % higher than either flank. At t = 120 the clamp is released. By t = 160 every cell has returned to X = 1. The chemistry is honest; the lesson is biology's actual problem (metabolism is continuous).

try this

  1. Default. Watch the two bright source disks; the streams meet at the midline within a few seconds. Read the gauges: midline fraction climbs above either half-grid fraction.
  2. Release sources. No further external input. The midline holds for a few seconds (chemistry resists), then drains. Compare R29 directly: same act, opposite outcome.
  3. Reclamp. Bring the sources back. The system re-fills. Communication is recoverable as long as you have working metabolism.
  4. v = 0 with clamp on. Source disks stay bright but the streams die at the source boundary. Pure local chemistry has no channel.
  5. v very high. Sources shoot mass into the midline so fast that high X reaches the opposite reservoir; the whole grid commits and the wave goes silent (ε high everywhere).

what changed vs. R29

R29 chain: latch_field → advect_by(label, piecewise vₓ) → latch_field → ε. The "permanent record" came from binary latches.

R29′ chain: react_field(X) → advect_by(X, piecewise vₓ) → ε. Source reservoirs supplied by external clamp. No latches. Same composition as R28′, position-dependent transport and a Dirichlet source -- no new operator added in this rung.
