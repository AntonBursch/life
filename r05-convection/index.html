R5 — Bénard convection

A box of fluid is heated at the bottom and cooled at the top. Below a threshold of heating, the heat just conducts through and the fluid stays still. Above the threshold, the fluid spontaneously organises into rolling cells that move heat much faster than conduction could. The structured state is better at dissipating than the unstructured one.

The slider is effectively a Rayleigh-number knob. The critical value for this geometry sits around g ≈ 0.01.

the claim

Below a critical amount of heating, this box does exactly what R1 promised: heat flows by diffusion, evenly, and the fluid never moves. Push the heating just past the threshold and something new happens. The fluid organises itself into rolling cells, and the heat now rides the cells through the box. The Nusselt number — the ratio of actual heat throughput to pure-conduction throughput — jumps from 1 to several. The structured state moves more heat than the boring state.

This is the first rung where pattern exists because it dissipates. The convection cells aren't sitting on top of the flow; they are the flow's way of doing its job faster. Patterns that out-dissipate the unstructured state are favoured. That's a seed of selection pressure with no genes and no chemistry, just hydrodynamics.

try this

  1. Start with no heat. Click no heat (g=0). The temperature settles into a smooth gradient from red at the bottom to blue at the top. Nu sits at 1.000. Nothing moves.
  2. Below threshold. Click below threshold. The box looks identical. Heating is on, but conduction handles it. Nu still ≈ 1. The system is being driven but has not organised.
  3. Near onset. Click near onset. Watch carefully — over a long time the perfectly flat gradient develops a faint wobble. The system is poised. Tiny perturbations refuse to die. Nu creeps above 1.
  4. Convection cells. Click convection cells. Now you can see them: hot plumes rising, cold plumes sinking, repeating along the box. Nu has jumped above 2 or 3. The structured state is moving real heat.
  5. Rolling boil. Click rolling boil. The cells become unstable, merge, split, drift. Nu can reach 5 or 6. The pattern is irregular but the throughput is high.
  6. Slide the heating slowly across the threshold. Watch Nu. It does not creep — it jumps. That jump is the rung. Below it, the box has no pattern. Above it, the box has one and works harder.

what to look for

  • Below threshold the box is dead. Conduction is enough; the fluid has no reason to move. You can put energy in all day and you will not get structure out.
  • Threshold is sharp. Nu sits at 1.0 until very close to onset, then jumps. The transition is qualitative, not gradual. A small change in input produces a categorical change in output.
  • The structured state out-transports the unstructured one. Same temperature difference top to bottom, more heat moving. The pattern is useful to the flow. This is the first rung where the universe has any reason to prefer pattern to no-pattern.

why this rung matters

R4 made patterns. R5 makes patterns that earn their keep. Above threshold, the convecting state dissipates the temperature gradient faster than the conducting state could. Out of two possible regimes, the universe lands on the one that processes the imposed flow more thoroughly.

This is selection without genes. No replicator, no memory, no copying — just a sharp boundary where the patterned state wins, every time, because it does the job of being a system better than the unpatterned state. Every rung above this one will reuse the move: when you push a substrate hard enough, it doesn't just respond — it reorganises into whatever pattern dissipates the push fastest. Living things are the most extreme example. They are also the most extreme dissipators.
